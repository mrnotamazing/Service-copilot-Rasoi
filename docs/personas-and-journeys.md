# Who TableMate is for: personas, journeys and a usability test

These are **proto-personas**: starting assumptions drawn from the project's pitch and the simulated restaurant (Saffron House), not findings from interviews. The usability test at the end is how to check them. Names match the demo's staff so what you show lines up with what you say.

## Personas

### Aisha, a server (Section A)
- **Does:** six tables at once; greets, orders, checks back, brings the bill, resets.
- **Knows:** every standard. Her problem is holding all of them in her head during a rush.
- **Fears:** being blamed for a slow kitchen; looking unprofessional in front of a guest.
- **Needs from TableMate:** the next three moves with a reason; a warning before the plate is made when there is an allergy; proof that a delay was not hers.
- **Would stop using it if:** it felt like surveillance, issued orders, or ranked her against colleagues.
- **Seen in:** Servers → her phone. Her private progress and practice stay on her screen.

### Priya, a server who works in two languages (Section F)
- **Does:** serves guests who speak Bengali and Spanish as well as English; reads the app in her own language.
- **Needs:** the cards, kudos and practice in her language; read-aloud when her hands are full; a manager who can pair her with guests who prefer her language.
- **Seen in:** Settings → language; Manager → Share the floor.

### Chef Vikram, the kitchen lead
- **Does:** runs the pass; tickets, timers, who is late, what is off the menu.
- **Fears:** being asked the same guest question ten times; a late ticket that servers do not know about.
- **Needs:** one answer to a guest question, saved for next time; a way to tell the whole floor "grill is backed up" in one note; a board to switch dishes off.
- **Seen in:** Kitchen pass.

### The floor manager
- **Does:** watches the room, steps in for unhappy tables and escalations, plans the next hour.
- **Fears:** finding out about a problem from an angry guest; unfairly marking down a server for the kitchen's delay.
- **Needs:** where help is needed now; why things are happening (kitchen or floor); a one-tap way to share a table; no leaderboard of people.
- **Seen in:** Manager.

## Journey: one table, one Saturday night

| Moment | Aisha does / feels | What goes wrong without TableMate | What TableMate does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seated | Six tables going; a new party sits at T4 | The greeting slips for a few minutes | A card: Greet T4, with the reason (seated 90 seconds ago, anniversary, one nut allergy) |
| Order fired | Relieved, moves to the next table | A dish with nuts goes to a guest with a nut allergy | The safety card becomes the top card; the kitchen ticket carries the same flag |
| Mains late | Does not know yet | The guest asks first and blames her | The kitchen's delay reaches her as a card, so she tells the table before they ask |
| Kitchen question | A guest asks about spice | She walks to the pass and asks, again | She taps ask; the chef answers once; the answer is saved for the next server |
| The bill | Has five other things to do | The guest waits | A card: bring the bill to T6, asked a minute ago |
| After service | Tired | A manager says she was slow | A receipt shows each step against its standard, coloured by whether the floor or the kitchen controlled it |
| Next week | Wants to get better | Retraining about things she knows | Private practice on the hard moments, with an honest score and an ideal reply |

## Hypotheses to test

1. Servers act on the top card without being told how, within the first minute.
2. A visible reason on each card builds trust; without it they ignore the cards.
3. Servers prefer suggestions to instructions and read "later" as permission, not as failure.
4. Managers use the kitchen/floor split to start a conversation with the kitchen, not to blame servers.
5. Practice is used in the quiet minutes only if it is private.

## Usability test script for the demo (about 20 minutes, 5 people per role)

Set up: Demo & setup → **Set up a presentation**. Ask the person to think aloud. Do not explain the screen first.

| # | Task | Success | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | As a server: "A guest has just sat at T4. What do you do?" | They find and complete the top card | Do they read the reason? Do they hesitate on what the button does? |
| 2 | "A guest says they cannot eat nuts. What happens when the order goes in?" | They notice the safety card and tell the kitchen | Do they trust it, or double-check by hand? |
| 3 | As the kitchen: "A guest has a question about the burrata. Answer it for next time." | They answer and tick save | Do they find the question? Do they understand "save to the menu"? |
| 4 | As the manager: "The mains for T2 are late. Whose problem is it?" | They find the kitchen/floor split and the receipt | Do they say it is the kitchen, or blame the server? |
| 5 | As the manager: "A guest at T4 does not speak English well. Fix it." | They hand the table to the right colleague | Do they find Share the floor without help? |
| 6 | As a server: "Practise telling a guest the dish they wanted is off." | They finish a practice and read the ideal reply | Do they feel judged? Is the score understood? |

After each role ask: What did you expect to happen? What was missing? Would you use this on a real shift, and what would make you stop?

Record: time to first correct action, wrong turns, quotes, and one thing they would change. Compare against the hypotheses above and update the personas.
